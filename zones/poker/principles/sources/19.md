# Bet Sizes

Your reaction to different bet sizes is a fundamental aspect of pre-flop and post-flop strategy.

## 1. Min-Raises (2 BB)
* **Context:** The minimum legal open-raise. Often used as a standard size in tournaments or by weaker players in cash games trying to "steal" cheaply.
* **Percentage of Pot:** Represents roughly a **133% pot-sized bet** pre-flop (raising 2 BB into a 1.5 BB pot of SB + BB).
* **Reaction:** Call with a wide range of hands, especially in late position or when defending the Big Blind due to the excellent pot odds. Consider 3-betting light if the opponent folds frequently to re-raises.

## 2. Standard Open Raises (2.2 – 3 BB)
* **Context:** The standard open-raise size in modern cash games. 
* **Percentage of Pot:** Represents roughly a **146% to 200% pot-sized bet** pre-flop.
* **Reaction:** Defend (call or 3-bet) with a standard, theoretically sound range based on your position and the opener’s position. If you choose to 3-bet, standard sizing is **3x to 4x** the original raise amount.

## 3. Large Raises (4 – 5 BB)
* **Context:** An abnormally large open, typically seen in live low-stakes games or from players adjusting to multiple limpers.
* **Percentage of Pot:** Represents a massive **266% to 333% pot-sized bet** pre-flop.
* **Reaction:** Tighten your calling range significantly. You do not need to defend as wide because the price to see a flop is worse. Lean toward 3-betting strictly premium value hands.

## 4. Very Large Raises (6 BB or more)
* **Context:** Extreme pre-flop open sizes, often indicating a recreational player with a strong but vulnerable hand (e.g., JJ, QQ, AK) trying to "protect" it, or an extremely loose-aggressive player.
* **Percentage of Pot:** Represents **400%+ of the pre-flop pot**.
* **Reaction:** Fold marginal hands and medium pocket pairs that rely on implied odds. Play highly selectively. If you have a premium hand (QQ+, AK), prefer calling to keep their bluffs in, or commit your stack via a 3-bet shove if stack depths allow.

## 5. Re-Raises (3-Bets)
* **Context:** An opponent raises over an initial open.
* **Percentage of Pot:** A standard 3-bet is typically **150% to 300%** of the original raise size.
* **Reaction:** 
  * If the 3-bet is small (under 3x your open), you can call wider.
  * If the 3-bet is standard (3x to 4x), fold your weakest opens, call with medium-strength hands that play well post-flop, and 4-bet with premiums or select bluffs.

## 6. All-In Bets
* **Context:** An opponent risks their entire stack.
* **Percentage of Pot:** An all-in bet can represent anywhere from a fraction of the pot to many times its size.
* **Reaction:** Assess your hand’s absolute equity versus their range, and calculate your exact pot odds. If the pot is bloated and you need low equity to break even, call wider; if they are shoving deep stacks over a small pot, fold all but your strongest premium holdings.

## Strategic Adjustments

* **Multi-Way Pots:** As more players enter the pot, the absolute pot size grows, but your individual share of equity decreases. Your calling range must tighten. The presence of extra players increases the likelihood that someone has a dominant hand, requiring you to play stronger, more robust ranges.
* **Stack Sizes:** Against short stacks (<25bb), prepare for high-variance, all-in confrontations. Against deep stacks (>100bb), prioritize hands with high implied odds (suited connectors, pocket pairs) that can win massive pots post-flop.
